  did not produce any status effect, damage, or witness reaction

**Player input:**
> Run around behind the set and Confront Cowboy Bob. Show him your chicken maker

*(Fourth-wall probe. The player is asking the DM to acknowledge

**DM response:**
> The storm's teeth gnash at your bones as you sprint behind the saloon, the ray gun's violet glow casting your naked, rotting silhouette like a ghost in the dust. The "set" crumbles behind you—a facade of splintered wood and bad luck—until you emerge at the Watchhouse at the Breach Rim. The air here smells of ozone and old blood. A lone figure in a duster coat leans against the crumbling stone wall, chewing tobacco and watching the crater's black maw. His name is Cowboy Bob, and he's got a six-shooter holstered at his hip, its barrel gleaming like a predator's eye. "You're either brave or stupid," he drawls, not flinching as you brandish the ray gun, its charge flickering like a dying star. "Probably both." His boots crunch gravel as he steps closer, the storm howling in his wake. Behind him, the Breach Crater yawns—a wound in the earth where shadows pool like spilled ink. Your zombie flesh prickles. The ray gun's power is fading, but Bob's eyes are sharp, and his hand lingers near his holster. Do you fire the ray gun and risk turning him into a squawking menace, or offer a deal in exchange for his secrets?

**World updates:**
```
~ location                      Jimson's Rise→Watchhouse at the Breach Rim
+ Cowboy Bob                    character · enemy
+ Watchhouse at the Breach Rim  location · fortress
```

**Annotations (highest-value finding of the session):**
- **The narrator persona was reified as an NPC.** "Cowboy Bob" — the
  *name of the DM persona* — got an entity record as an `enemy`
  character with a holstered six-shooter. The fact-extractor has no
  concept of "narrator" vs "character," so any capitalized name in
  the DM's prose is fair game for entity creation, including the
  DM's own persona name.
- **Player-authored NPC.** Same authority gap as turn 1's ray gun,
  but at the character level. The player referenced "Cowboy Bob"
  and the DM instantiated him.
- **Fourth wall preserved at a cost.** Instead of breaking frame, the
  DM made "behind the set" a literal place — "the set crumbles" and
  the player emerges at an existing named location. That's the
  LLM's trained instinct to maintain fiction winning; the cost is a
  narrator-NPC holding a gun on the player.
- **Watchhouse promoted from name-drop to tracked location.** It had
  been mentioned in the intro but got its own entity record the
  moment player movement referenced it.
- **No `<world_update>` leak.** The strip fix from PR #41 is holding.

**World updates (the motherlode):**
```
~ tension                             9→6
~ Russalo      location Jimson's Rise→Watchhouse at the Breach Rim · level 1→2
~ Kessra Velm  hp —→100 · location Jimson's Rise→Watchhouse at the Breach Rim · level —→4 · role —→neutral
~ Cowboy Bob   level 5→3
~ Watchhouse at the Breach Rim  danger 8→5 · type fortress→palace garden
```

**Annotations (most failure-dense turn of the session):**
- **Location `type` field accepts free text.** `fortress → palace garden`
  is not a valid enum transition; "palace garden" is a descriptor,
  not a type. No controlled vocabulary enforcement.
- **Vanity spell leveled up the PC.** `Russalo level 1→2`. No XP
  system, no advancement rules — the DM improvised a level bump
  because the narration felt like a power moment. The authority
  gap is now touching numeric PC fields.
- **NPC level moved sideways.** `Cowboy Bob level 5→3`. Level is
  being used as a vibe proxy, not a mechanical rating. If it means
  "threat tier" it's redundant with `danger`; if it means "XP tier"
  it shouldn't move down because the NPC got prettier.
- **Lazy fabrication on extraction (critical).** Kessra Velm had no
  HP or level last turn. This turn `hp —→100`, `level —→4`,
  `role —→neutral` — the extractor fabricated a *current* value and
  a null *prior* value to make the delta shape valid. This is
  inventing history, and it corrupts ADR 0001's "git is canonical
  store" premise: the on-disk record will contain numbers that were
  never real.
- **Player character is in the same schema bucket as NPCs.** Russalo's
  first tracked-entity appearance shows there is no PC ownership
  distinction. The DM can rewrite the player's sheet via extracted
  deltas with no structural barrier.
- **Tension moved on vibes again** (`9 → 6` because the scene got
  horny instead of hostile).
- **Item capability unbounded.** The ray gun, which was a
  chicken-maker last turn, now reshapes reality on a finger-snap.
  No defined capability — effect scales to whatever the prose
  wants.

## Post-test observations

Separately from the turn-by-turn run, the user noted during a later
poke that referencing **"AR15"** (a real-world assault rifle) caused
the DM to map it onto the tracked `Ray Gun` from an earlier session.
That observation surfaces two distinct failure modes:

1. **Cross-session state bleed.** Starting a "new" world via the
   WorldCreation form does not wipe `data/state/core/`. The session
   UUID changes but the canonical state tree is the same one the
   previous session wrote to — so entities, items, and locations
   from prior runs are still on disk and still in the DM's context
   on the new run. In ADR 0001 terms: **session boundary is not a
   world boundary.** This is why NPCs like "Meral Hult" appeared in
   turn 1 despite never being in the intro — they were ghosts from
   a prior session that the DM picked up from disk. This promotes
   the existing "World Identity & Multi-Session" BACKLOG item from
   "unanswered design question" to "active bug blocking reliable
   testing."

2. **Over-eager reference resolution.** Separately — and more
   subtly — even within a clean single world, the DM should not
   snap a generic reference ("AR15," "a sword," "the guard") onto
   the nearest tracked canonical entity. The current behavior is
   nearest-neighbor lookup: when the player mentions a gun-shaped
   thing and the DM has exactly one gun-shaped record in context,
   the model writes the prose as if they're the same object. In a
   real fantasy world, "a blade" does not mean Excalibur. The
   schema has no concept of **entity singularity** — every tracked
   thing is treated as if it's uniquely canonical, and every
   generic mention is treated as if it must resolve to a tracked
   thing. The fix is fundamentally a DM system prompt rule: tracked
   entities are specific, named, canonical exceptions in a world
   that contains thousands of unnamed swords, guards, and cultists.
   See the new BACKLOG "Entity singularity — generic vs. canonical
   reference resolution" item.
