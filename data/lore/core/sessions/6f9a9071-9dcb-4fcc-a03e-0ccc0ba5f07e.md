The dreams are free,” and you notice the bell rope is missing from the church steeple outside, though the bell itself is there, rusted shut.  

Upstairs, the Pell family’s laughter rises from the mill, syncopated, as if each syllable is borrowed and must be returned. Their millstones grind something that smells of iron and old names. The inn’s floorboards creak when no one treads them. You find a letter on your pillow, sealed with wax the color of a fresh bruise. The handwriting is yours.
